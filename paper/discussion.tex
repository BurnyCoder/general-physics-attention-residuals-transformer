\section{Discussion, claims, and implications}
The leading radiative-flow result is an observed mean ranking: Block achieves twelve-step NMSE 0.28917, below Standard (0.39277), Full (0.31735), Uniform Block (0.34735), persistence (0.41901), and every initialized model. Relative to the arithmetic mean Standard and persistence errors, the reductions are 26.38\% and 30.99\%. Restricting trained architectures to shared seeds 0--2 preserves Block's lowest mean (0.29203 versus 0.40246, 0.31735, and 0.34735 for Standard, Full, and Uniform). This is not uniform seed-level dominance: Full slightly beats Block in seed zero. It is a descriptive highlight chosen after results, not a new confirmatory superiority test against all comparators\cite{studycode}.

The experiment separates a practical architecture comparison from a mechanistic question. A Standard--Block difference estimates the total effect of replacing residual composition under the matched training protocol. The Uniform Block control helps assess whether learned selection contributes beyond the change to normalized averaging, but its three-seed sample is exploratory. Interventions on trained Block weights examine immediate routing dependence while retaining the learned backbone; they are not interchangeable with training a uniform-routing model from scratch.

All six paired seed log ratios favor Block over Standard. The estimated 24.52\% reduction and interval [11.56,35.58]\% support the prespecified 5\% threshold under the stated conditional inference assumptions. The exact $p=0.03125$ is the smallest attainable two-sided value with six pairs, not evidence of high-resolution tail estimation. This primary result concerns the geometric combination of dataset error ratios; it is not a 24.52\% reduction in every field or dataset.

\subsection{Relative gains and absolute forecasting failures}
At twelve steps, arithmetic mean seed NMSE is 39,284 for Standard and 30,006 for Block on convection, compared with 0.2174 for persistence. Every trained variant loses decisively to persistence there, including the exploratory controls. On the radiative layer the corresponding Standard, Block, and persistence errors are 0.3928, 0.2892, and 0.4190. At twenty-four steps, Block's radiative error is 0.4270 versus Standard's 0.7028 and persistence's 0.5403. Relative improvement over a weak learned baseline must therefore be distinguished from a useful physics surrogate.

A post-hoc decomposition of the already saved, prespecified seed-zero first convection example clarifies one metric sensitivity without changing the endpoint. For Standard, the first-twelve-step per-field NMSE values are 0.502 for pressure, 0.0394 for buoyancy, 15,707 for horizontal velocity, and 34,062 for vertical velocity. The minimum standardized velocity target energies in this window are $1.19\times10^{-7}$ and $2.67\times10^{-8}$, below the fixed $10^{-6}$ stabilizer. Consequently, modest standardized velocity errors receive very large NMSE weights. The independent decomposition exactly reproduces that saved window's aggregate\cite{studycode}. This single fixed example explains an observed contribution, not every large convection error. Epsilon, windows, and field weights were not changed after observing it.

All evaluated forecasts remain finite through 24 steps. Finite arithmetic does not establish physical stability: the mean convection divergence error is 4.134 for Standard, 2.823 for Block, and 0.228 for persistence. Radiative negative-density fractions are 26.04\% for Standard and 27.30\% for Block, versus zero for persistence, averaged over all 24 forecast steps and the specified trajectories. Thus the NMSE gain does not improve this positivity diagnostic. No negative-pressure cells were recorded in these ordinary evaluations. No clipping or positivity repair is applied.

\subsection{Exploratory controls and routing dependence}
Using only matched seeds 0--2 and the same equal-dataset log weighting, Full's geometric error ratio to Block is 0.8349, while Uniform Block's is 1.0238. The three Uniform/Block paired log ratios include both signs. These exploratory observations do not establish that learning depth weights is necessary for the primary Standard--Block gain; normalized source aggregation is also changed by that comparison.

On the fixed diagnostic subset, replacing trained Block routing with uniform weights increases twelve-step error by mean factors 2.565 on convection and 7.490 on radiative flow. Shuffling depth weights gives factors 2.630 and 9.524. Every tested seed's factor exceeds one for each of these comparisons. The precision-matched interventions demonstrate dependence on routing in those trained models and selected trajectories. They do not identify a unique mediator of the training-time architecture effect, and the subset's manifest ordering does not cover all parameter settings.

\subsection{What the initialization-only controls reveal}
The later user-requested controls compare reconstructed initial states and final checkpoints for each original architecture/seed identity on the same complete test population. Training reduces twelve-step radiative-flow error for all eighteen identities. On convection, Standard improves for all six seeds, but Block, Full, and Uniform Block worsen in all six, three, and three seeds, respectively. Their trained-to-initialized ratios of arithmetic mean convection NMSE are 3.671, 13.812, and 3.211. The corresponding Block means are 8,173.50 at initialization and 30,005.97 after training; Full's are 1,332.00 and 18,397.80. Thus final-checkpoint superiority to Standard does not establish improvement over these architectures' own initial states on convection. Large architecture differences already exist before learning. These are descriptive post-hoc findings, not new confirmatory tests; they neither change the endpoint nor identify a unique cause of degradation\cite{studycode}.

Block and Uniform Block have identical initialized tensor hashes and bitwise-identical saved forecasts for their shared seeds 0--2. Their reported overall initialized means differ because Block also includes seeds 3--5. Identical initial states must not be counted as independent evidence for a learned-routing benefit. All four architectures, initialized or trained, still lose to persistence on convection under the fixed metric. The held-out initial-state overlap and low-energy metric sensitivity apply equally to these new controls.

\subsection{Measured cost}
All eighteen runs finished 3,175 updates, with no failed or omitted training runs. Training consumed 11.068 GPU-phase hours, below its 18-hour allocation and the pretraining 14.4-hour projection. Mean per-run wall times are 1,570 seconds for Standard, 2,460 for Block, 2,756 for Full, and 2,455 for Uniform. Mean run-level throughputs are respectively 34.42, 21.19, 19.09, and 21.41 examples per second; PyTorch peak allocated memory is 278.46, 365.88, 546.00, and 345.86 MiB. These are measurements on one laptop GPU, including varying host conditions, not universal kernel benchmarks. Equal optimizer updates match data exposure while leaving compute cost unequal.

\section{Limitations}
Six independent pairs constrain both power and the precision of variance estimates. Three-seed controls cannot support broad systematic claims. The confidence interval is conditional on selected trajectories and its normality approximation; no claim of coverage under arbitrary effect distributions is made. Matching optimizer updates does not match wall-clock compute because routing architectures have different costs. Conversely, equal compute would change the data exposure and is not the primary comparison specified here.

The routing-intervention subset is chosen by manifest order and is not balanced over every parameter setting. Its immediate within-model effects cannot establish that routing explains behavior across all held-out regimes. Descriptive secondary intervals are unadjusted and do not replace the preregistered primary test.

All test trajectories share their initial state with training at a different physical parameter setting, although neither complete trajectories nor their first four prepared frames are exact cross-split duplicates. The official split therefore does not establish generalization to entirely unseen initial conditions. Initial-state reuse and related simulation families also limit extrapolation beyond this fixed benchmark; the seed-level inference is conditional on it.

Coarsening removes unresolved spatial structure and changes numerical divergence residuals. Training-standardized NMSE can strongly weight low-energy targets despite the fixed stabilizer. The model learns a mixture of temporal strides without an explicit stride token, preserving the upstream forward interface; this may limit temporal identifiability. A 12-step endpoint is not a guarantee of asymptotic dynamical stability. GPU execution can vary across hardware and library versions even with deterministic algorithms requested\cite{pytorchrepro}. All these limitations apply regardless of the sign of the observed effect.

\section{Conclusion}
GPhyT with Block Attention Residuals has the lowest mean twelve-step radiative-flow error among all nine tested trained, initialized, and persistence comparators. Its mean error is 26.38\% lower than GPhyT without Attention Residuals and 30.99\% lower than persistence. This descriptive ranking is restricted to the tested population and does not establish significance against each comparator.

Block Attention Residuals reduces the prespecified paired error endpoint relative to Standard GPhyT in this completed 18-run study. That evidence is conditional on two selected official splits, 128-by-64 resolution, GPhyT-S, and 3,175 updates per run. It coexists with severe convection failure against persistence and frequent unphysical radiative density predictions. The controls leave the incremental benefit of learning routing over fixed averaging unresolved, while within-model interventions show routing dependence on their fixed subset. These findings justify a bounded architecture result and targeted future hypotheses, not claims of solver reliability or general physics generalization.
The additional initialization-only baselines also show convection degradation after training for all three routing variants. This failure is visible despite their favorable final-checkpoint comparison with Standard.
